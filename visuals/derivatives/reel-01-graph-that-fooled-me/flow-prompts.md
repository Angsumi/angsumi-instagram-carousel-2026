# Reel 01 — The graph that fooled me

Three separate 10-second vertical clips. Upload the matching PNG as the image reference for each Flow generation. Keep the same woman, desk, maroon sweater, lamp, laptop, and orange/blue mock graph across all clips. The graph is illustrative, not research data. Add captions in your editor so they stay readable.

## 00–10s — Hook

Reference: `segment-01-00-10s.png`

**Flow prompt:** Static close desk shot with a gentle push toward the laptop. The researcher studies the attractive graph, then her expression shifts from impressed to skeptical. Keep her face, clothing, lighting, background, and graph exactly like the reference. Natural documentary movement; no new text or objects.

**Voice/on-screen:** “This graph looks impressive. But what am I actually looking at?”

## 10–20s — Reveal the problem

Reference: `segment-02-10-20s.png`

**Flow prompt:** Maintain the same desk and camera position. The researcher leans closer and points once to the blank axis area of the laptop graph. The camera slowly pushes in so the missing labels become obvious. Keep the synthetic graph unchanged; no added numbers or scientific claims.

**Voice/on-screen:** “No clear labels. No units. No context.”

## 20–30s — Useful takeaway

Reference: `segment-03-20-30s.png`

**Flow prompt:** Same woman and setting. She holds up the cream notebook with three empty checkbox squares, gives a small knowing smile, and glances back to the graph. Keep the notebook squares empty for captions added later. Natural hand movement, steady vertical frame; no generated writing.

**Voice/on-screen:** “Before sharing a graph, check the labels, units, and what the viewer needs to know.”

**End card / CTA:** “Could someone outside your lab read it? Save this check.”
